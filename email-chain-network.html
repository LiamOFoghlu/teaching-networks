What the measures mean

Density
The proportion of possible ties that actually exist: the number of ties divided by the number of pairs. It runs from 0, where nobody is connected to anybody, to 1, where everybody is connected to everybody. Density falls as a group gets larger, because the number of possible pairs grows far faster than anyone's capacity for contacts, so it is only comparable between networks of similar size.
Mean path
Take the shortest route between two people, count the steps, and average that over every pair in the network. It says how far apart people typically are once you allow for going through other people.
Diameter
The longest of those shortest routes: the number of steps between the two people furthest apart. It is the worst case rather than the typical one, so it tells you how far something would have to travel to reach the whole network.
Clustering
How often two people who share a contact are also connected to each other, averaged across everyone. It measures closed triangles. A chain passed from hand to hand produces almost none, so any clustering here was created when two chains crossed.

Betweenness and closeness are computed on the undirected union of all four chains; the tie between Dáin and Dori carries two chains and counts once.
