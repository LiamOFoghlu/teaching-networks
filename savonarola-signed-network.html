Coded from Lauro Martines, Fire in the City: Savonarola and the Struggle for the Soul of Renaissance Florence (2006). Martines does not tabulate guilds or quarters, so the collectives are those his narrative identifies: factions, leading families, religious orders, foreign powers and broad social groups. Families are coded by the members he names, so a split family can carry positive ties to both camps. A triad is balanced when the product of its three signs is positive ("the friend of my friend is my friend", "the enemy of my enemy is my friend"). Centrality treats the active ties as unsigned and undirected; betweenness is normalised to 0–1 and closeness is harmonic closeness. In the pooled view, a pair whose tie changed sign over time keeps its latest sign. The random baseline is the share of balanced triads expected if the same number of positive and negative ties were assigned at random: p³ + 3p(1 − p)².
